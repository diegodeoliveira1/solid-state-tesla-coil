# Project background and team credits

## From Physics class to a finished build

In a Physics course with Professor Webber, a video of a human-sized Tesla coil inspired Diego Deoliveira to research whether a smaller, lower-power design could be assembled. Diego and Owen Trafton discussed starting the project the previous year, but it was never completed. When an open-ended final project became available in ENCE 3100 Electronics at the University of Denver, the original idea became the basis of a real electronics build.

The class project team was **Diego Deoliveira and Dirk Bosgraaf**. **Owen Trafton**, a Mechanical Engineering major, also assisted with hands-on work and assembly.

The team's goals were to apply knowledge of resistors, capacitors, diodes, and MOSFETs; assemble physical primary and secondary windings; create a functioning compact solid-state Tesla coil; and document the challenges and results in a final presentation.

## What the completed project represents

The team built an approximately one-foot-tall resonant coil that generated a recorded high-voltage discharge. The project also documented real engineering difficulties—coil winding, solder reliability, correct electrical connections, and a reported MOSFET failure in a separate test. Both the success and the failure are part of the project's technical record.
